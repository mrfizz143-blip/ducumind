France — History

The history of France is one of the most influential in European and world history. The territory of modern France was inhabited by the Gauls, a Celtic people, before the Roman conquest. Julius Caesar conquered Gaul between 58 and 50 BC, and the region became part of the Roman Empire.

After the fall of Rome, the Frankish king Clovis unified much of the region under the Merovingian dynasty around 486 AD, adopting Christianity and establishing the kingdom that would become France. Charlemagne, crowned emperor in 800 AD, expanded Frankish rule across much of Western Europe.

The medieval period saw the construction of cathedrals such as Notre-Dame de Paris and the outbreak of the Hundred Years' War against England from 1337 to 1453. The French Revolution of 1789 overthrew the monarchy and declared the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, reshaping modern political thought worldwide.

Napoleon Bonaparte rose to power in the aftermath of the Revolution and crowned himself Emperor in 1804, spreading the French legal and administrative system, including the Napoleonic Code, across Europe.

The 19th and 20th centuries were turbulent, including the Franco-Prussian War, two World Wars, and the establishment of the Fifth Republic in 1958 under Charles de Gaulle. Modern France remains a global power with significant cultural, scientific, and economic influence.

Paris has served as the capital of France for centuries and continues to be the political and cultural heart of the nation. The city played a central role in the revolutions and wars that shaped modern France, from the storming of the Bastille in 1789 to its liberation in 1944.
