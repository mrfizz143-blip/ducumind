France — Geography and Overview

France, officially the French Republic, is a country primarily located in Western Europe. It is the largest country in the European Union by land area, covering approximately 551,695 square kilometres. France shares land borders with Belgium, Luxembourg, Germany, Switzerland, Italy, Spain, and Andorra, and is connected to the United Kingdom through the Channel Tunnel.

The capital of France is Paris, which is situated in the north-central part of the country on the banks of the River Seine. Paris is the largest city in France and is globally recognised as a centre for art, fashion, gastronomy, and culture.

The geography of France is remarkably diverse. The country features the Alps and the Pyrenees mountain ranges, the Massif Central plateau, extensive river systems including the Loire, Rhône, Seine, and Garonne, and a long Atlantic coastline along the Bay of Biscay. The highest point in France is Mont Blanc in the Alps, which reaches 4,809 metres above sea level.

France has a temperate climate, with oceanic influences in the west, continental influences inland, and Mediterranean conditions along the southern coast. Agriculture remains a significant sector, and France is one of the world's largest producers of wine and wheat.

The population of France was estimated at around 68 million people in recent years. French is the official language, and the country operates under a semi-presidential republic with the President as head of state and the Prime Minister as head of government.

The euro is the official currency of France. The country is a founding member of the European Union, the United Nations, and NATO, and is one of the five permanent members of the United Nations Security Council.

Key facts: Capital — Paris; Largest city — Paris; Official language — French; Currency — Euro; Government — Unitary semi-presidential republic; Highest point — Mont Blanc at 4,809 m.
